# Both Hands V05.04: Kinetic Mobile

## Question

Can twelve spatial records become a suspended kinetic archive whose final form retains a small, legible memory of bodily stability?

## Change from V05.00

V05.04 keeps the raw two-hand midpoint coordinates and chronological record order while extending the session to twelve cycles. For the displayed mobile, the central camera working range is expanded across a wider exhibition field rather than shown as a one-to-one screen coordinate. It removes the nested contour and planar constellation language. Each record becomes a suspension point with a thin line and translucent counterweight plate; the twelve records are assembled through an irregular hierarchy of aged-brass rods.

## Interaction

Begin with both hands close together. Open, move the full two-hand stretch through camera space, pause if desired, then return. The average midpoint sampled while the hands are widely open fixes the suspension point. Each new record gives the complete structure a small impulse before it gradually returns to equilibrium.

`P` cycles the hand display through **Points → Skeleton → Hidden**. `R` clears the mobile. `?` opens the field guide. Appending `?demo` to the URL creates a complete twelve-record reference mobile without camera access.

## Data mapping

- Average two-hand midpoint while widely open → raw suspension record, remapped from the central camera working range into the wider exhibition composition
- Signed hand tilt → counterweight plate angle
- Movement stability → initial swing amplitude (less stable movement produces a larger swing)
- One uninterrupted open-palm hold → five counterweight sizes; slow opening frames do not count, and the largest tier is reached in under one second of stillness
- Completion order remains visible as the immutable record number 01—12, but is no longer presented as a visual data mapping

Slowness and duration remain recorded in each memory object for archive continuity, but they do not change the requested final material mapping.

## Kinetic behaviour

- A new record introduces a low-frequency impulse across every existing record.
- Each suspension point uses a damped spring with slightly different frequency, so the mobile moves as one object without repeating mechanically.
- The newest, less stable record carries the largest local impulse.
- Damping gradually returns all offsets and plate rotations to their original mapped coordinates and angles.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` removes the entry impulse and settles the structure immediately.
